The study

From neural responses to object-space geometry

Factor analysis of 7 T fMRI responses revealed a high-dimensional object representation dominated by two latent axes. Together, they accounted for over half of the explainable variance and organised object concepts along continuous gradients.

Design and analysis. Twenty participants completed repeated object-recognition sessions. The stimulus collection spanned 1,854 THINGS concepts; the main factor analysis used 960 WordNet-labelled concepts organised into 14 high-level categories. A 50-factor solution was selected by cross-validation, with the two dominant axes used to characterise large-scale geometry.
Main findings

Two axes, three poles and a cortical gradient

01 / FACTOR 1

Biological salience

One axis tracks an animacy-related continuum, extending beyond animals to inanimate objects with human-like features. It is not simply a binary animate-versus-inanimate label.

02 / FACTOR 2

Scene context and action affordance

A second axis brings together real-world scale, scene associations and manipulability. Furniture and vehicles occupy one extreme, while smaller, more readily handled objects tend towards the other.

03 / GEOMETRY

Animal · furniture · food

Animal-, furniture- and food-related concepts anchor a triangular object space. Other categories occupy overlapping regions along its edges and interior.

Neural implementation and replication. The factors map onto a gradual lateral-to-medial cortical organisation, from regions overlapping face-selective cortex towards scene-selective cortex. The broad representational geometry is stable across participants and generalises to independent THINGS-fMRI and CNeuroMod-THINGS datasets.
